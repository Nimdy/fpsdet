# FPSDET Benchmark v1

Generated by `fpsdet benchmark report` from the committed artifacts (fpsdet.benchmark/1; release manifest `benchmark/release.json`, fpsdet.benchmark-release/1). Do not edit by hand: change the artifacts, then regenerate.

FPSDET Benchmark v1 is a version of the benchmark: its datasets, selections, draws and expectations. It is not a version of fpsdet, whose own version (0.3.0 at this release) changes independently; a later fpsdet is checked against the same benchmark and reported as MATCH, PROVENANCE_ONLY or DRIFT.

## What this benchmark can prove

- That the committed numbers follow from the committed artifacts: every result, evaluation or per-draw statistics file, strength estimate, split and spread verifies by digest, and its numbers recompute, without re-scoring anything.
- That fpsdet's decisions on the TF2 and CS2 datasets follow from the prepared inputs: the same events, labels, cohort and profile give the same cases, packets, eligibility, evaluation and strength, on Python 3.11 and 3.12.
- That the prepared inputs follow from the frozen sources by public rules, with no private key: anyone holding the same downloads rebuilds tf2-rgl-v2's data and all nine baseline draws, and cs2cd-v2's data from its one pinned revision, byte for byte. A download that differs from the frozen source is reported as SOURCE_CHANGED, part by part, never used as if it were the same.
- Exactly what changed when anything moves: the upstream source (SOURCE_CHANGED, with the digest pinned and found), the selection, the code, the ids, the inputs, labels, profile or cohort, the detector code, decisions, findings, eligibility, statistics, strength, or only presentation.
- How much a TF2 number depends on which never-banned players build the baseline: nine draws fixed before any was run, all reported, none preferred.
- That every native detector trips on a behaviour planted for it and stays quiet on an honest twin, and that the challenge and external-signature protocols behave as documented, in controlled synthetic fixtures.

## What this benchmark cannot prove

- How well fpsdet detects cheating in general. Each real dataset is one population with someone else's labels: RGL's bans and the CS2CD authors' judgement, neither of them ground truth.
- Anything about a detector a dataset cannot observe. On TF2 and CS2 most detectors are not observable at all; that is a gap in the data, not a zero.
- That a TF2 number is a fixed property of fpsdet. It moves with the baseline draw; the benchmark reports the median and the range over nine draws, and nine draws show the spread, not its limits.
- That upstream will keep serving the frozen sources. logs.tf and RGL can edit or remove what was fetched; the digests detect it (SOURCE_CHANGED) but cannot restore it, and nothing downloaded is committed.
- That synthetic fixtures resemble real cheats. They are planted by construction and qualify code, never a rate.
- That fpsdet is better or worse than any other system. Nothing else is evaluated here.

There is no overall score. Each section answers its own question, and a detector a dataset cannot observe is shown as not observable, never as zero.

Every statement below carries its class, and no sentence mixes two:

- **[controlled fixture]** planted by construction in synthetic fixtures and scenarios; it qualifies code, never a rate.
- **[benchmark v1 real data]** measured on a current dataset of this release, rebuildable from public sources without any private key.
- **[historical real data]** a run published before this release, still verifiable, superseded, and never the benchmark result.
- **[architecture limit]** what no fpsdet check can see, by design or by a controlled scenario that gets through.
- **[unmeasured]** what the benchmark's data cannot show at all, which is not the same as finding nothing.

**TF2 league matches with RGL ban labels** (`tf2-rgl-v2`).
- Source: [logs.tf](https://logs.tf): match logs that TF2 community servers upload: each player's shots and hits per weapon, sniper headshots, and kills per minute; [RGL](https://rgl.gg): the competitive league's public ban list, with a reason for each ban. Fetched 2026-10-04, frozen at 2026-10-03. RGL's ban list grows and its reasons can be edited, and a player's logs.tf match list grows with every match. The selection reads only bans dated on or before the freeze and matches played on or before it, so a later download selects the same accounts and matches, unless upstream changed what it already held: an edited or removed ban, or a removed or re-uploaded log. prepare reports that as SOURCE_CHANGED, with the digest it expected and the one it found, and never builds on it silently.
- Licence and use: Neither site publishes terms for its data. logs.tf's robots.txt asks crawlers to stay off its API, so the fetcher asks for a bounded sample, one or two requests at a time with a pause after each, names this project in its User-Agent, and caches every response. Nothing downloaded is committed, and nothing per player: this dataset's ids are public hashes of SteamIDs, so only aggregate numbers and digests are published. No log, ban, SteamID, name, match id, per-player row, case or split list. Data: logs.tf match logs and RGL's public bans. Not affiliated with Valve, logs.tf or RGL.
- Archive: manifest and digests only: neither logs.tf nor RGL grants redistribution, so a rebuild must fetch from upstream. The digests say exactly whether it got the same data.
- Ids: None, on purpose. Each SteamID3 becomes tfb- and the first 12 hex digits of SHA-256("fpsdet.tf2-benchmark-identity/1", NUL, SteamID3). Anyone can compute it, so a third party's rebuild has the same ids and the same baseline draws without any key. Because anyone holding a SteamID can also find its id, nothing keyed by it is published. A benchmark identity is not pseudonymization. A deployment that stores or shares player ids should use a keyed hash under a secret key, as tf2logs.py does, never this public one.
- Selection, frozen (`fpsdet.benchmark-selection/tf2-rgl/2`): Freeze: RGL bans dated after 2026-10-03 are not bans here, and no match played after it is selected. Labelled accounts: accounts RGL banned for cheating (not for assisting, distributing or developing cheats) with at least 20 team matches on logs.tf before the ban; their 20 latest team matches before the ban. Extra never-banned players: everyone seen at least 3 times in those matches whom RGL had not banned, taken in the order of SHA-256("fpsdet.tf2-honest-order/1", NUL, benchmark identity) until 300 have at least 20 team matches up to the freeze; their 20 latest. Comparison: every never-banned player with at least 8 matches among the selected ones, which includes the 300 extra players. Equal evidence: every player keeps at most their 20 latest selected matches; a banned account's only before its ban. A team match has 12 or more players. Only aimed, hitscan weapons are converted. No private key is read anywhere: the ids, the extra players and every baseline draw follow from public hashes.
- Counts: bans 3,457, labelled accounts 228, extra never banned 300, selected logs 10,149.

**CS2 matchmaking (CS2CD) with the dataset authors' labels** (`cs2cd-v2`).
- Source: [CS2CD on Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/CS2CD/CS2CD.Counter-Strike_2_Cheat_Detection): 795 CS2 matchmaking matches parsed from server-recorded demos, with the authors' cheater labels. Fetched 2026-10-06, revision `44e5129654508b22802a050a45bcdbb44b103d87`. Pinned: every fetch reads revision 44e5129654508b22802a050a45bcdbb44b103d87 (2025-05-19) and nothing else; if the hub no longer has it, the fetch stops with SOURCE_CHANGED instead of taking the latest. Each event file is checked byte for byte against its git blob id, and each tick table's size and LFS SHA-256 as the hub reports them at that revision (byte for byte with cs2fetch.py --full). The cached tick tables are rewritten by pyarrow, so only the event files, which the fetcher writes deterministically, are part of the prepared source identity.
- Licence and use: CS2CD is published under CC BY 4.0. Nothing downloaded is committed. Per-player evaluation rows with the converter's match-local ids, and aggregate numbers, are. Data: CS2CD by its authors, CC BY 4.0. Not affiliated with Valve or with the dataset's authors.
- Archive: raw source legally redistributable: CC BY 4.0 allows copies with attribution. None is kept here; the pinned revision on the hub is the archive, and the source list's digests say whether a fetch got it.
- Ids: CS2CD names players Player_N within each match; the converter writes them as <match>-p<N> (nc047-p9). There is no key, so a rebuild gets the same ids. A player cannot be followed across matches.
- Selection, frozen (`fpsdet.benchmark-selection/cs2cd/2`): Revision: 44e5129654508b22802a050a45bcdbb44b103d87 of CS2CD, and no other. Matches: the first 180 no-cheater and the first 120 with-cheater matches by number, keeping complete ones (13 rounds or more). Baseline: the first 120 complete no-cheater matches, screened for whole lobbies of outliers by fpsdet baseline --screen-matches. Their players are never scored. Scored: the other 45 complete no-cheater matches (unreviewed) and the 108 complete with-cheater matches (hand-labelled cheaters and hand-reviewed players). Every player is one match.
- Counts: matches fetched 300, complete matches 273, events 4,071,829, scored players 1,529, baseline players 887; labelled cheater 504, clean, reviewed match 576, clean, unreviewed match 1,649.

## 10. Known blind spots

- **[architecture limit]** An assist that copies human timing, human noise and human lag.
- **[architecture limit]** An aim assist tuned to stay inside the best humans measured: past every human is a finding, better than average is not.
- **[architecture limit]** A reader that follows each challenge only briefly, only in the last second of its window, or only beside an enemy the client can see.
- **[architecture limit]** A cheat that waits out the interpolation delay.
- **[architecture limit]** A capture-card aimbot looking at pixels.
- **[architecture limit]** A DMA read of the frame the game actually drew.
- **[architecture limit]** A wallhack that never aims at a hidden enemy.
- **[architecture limit]** Shared positions acted on no faster than a voice could carry them, or shared by a player who is not in review.
- **[unmeasured]** Anything a game's telemetry cannot show. In the benchmark's real data, 17 of 22 detectors on TF2 and 15 of 22 detectors on CS2 are not observable at all, which is not the same as finding nothing.
- **[architecture limit]** A number with too few humans behind it: a detector waits for a thick enough baseline, and says so, before it compares.
- **[architecture limit]** A server that labels a visible enemy as hidden manufactures cases. That is an emitter bug, and the docs call it out field by field.
- **[architecture limit]** External records are read unsigned by default, and a verified signature proves who signed a record, not that its finding is true.

## 11. Reproduce it

Everything below runs from a clone of the repository with standard-library Python 3.11 or 3.12. The CS2 fetcher and converter also need `pip install -r examples/cs2/requirements.txt`; the external-signature scenarios need `pip install 'fpsdet[auth]'`. No step needs a key, an account or anything private.

```bash
# Check every committed artifact, offline, without scoring (what CI runs):
PYTHONPATH=src python -m fpsdet benchmark verify
# Rebuild and qualify every controlled fixture and scenario (seconds, no data):
PYTHONPATH=src python -m fpsdet benchmark run --dataset synthetic
# CS2: fetch the one pinned revision (network, about 4.3 GB), rebuild the inputs and run (offline):
PYTHONPATH=src python -m fpsdet benchmark fetch --dataset cs2 --data ~/fpsdet-data/cs2 --python <a Python with the CS2 requirements>
PYTHONPATH=src python -m fpsdet benchmark prepare --dataset cs2 --data ~/fpsdet-data/cs2 --python <the same>
PYTHONPATH=src python -m fpsdet benchmark run --dataset cs2 --data cs2=~/fpsdet-data/cs2
# TF2: fetch the frozen sources (network, rate-limited, hours), rebuild all nine draws and run them (offline):
PYTHONPATH=src python -m fpsdet benchmark fetch --dataset tf2 --data ~/fpsdet-data/tf2
PYTHONPATH=src python -m fpsdet benchmark prepare --dataset tf2 --data ~/fpsdet-data/tf2
PYTHONPATH=src python -m fpsdet benchmark run --dataset tf2 --data tf2=~/fpsdet-data/tf2
```

prepare checks the download against the frozen source first and says SOURCE_CHANGED, with the digest it expected and the one it found, if upstream moved; it never builds on a changed download as if it were the same. Each run prints MATCH, ENVIRONMENT_ONLY, PROVENANCE_ONLY, PRESENTATION_ONLY, DRIFT, INPUT_CHANGED or NOT_RUN per dataset (per draw, for TF2), with the categories that explain it, and writes every artifact beside the data.

Ordinary CI runs `benchmark verify`, the synthetic class and the report check on every change. A manual workflow (`.github/workflows/benchmark-cs2.yml`) rebuilds CS2 from an empty cache at the pinned revision. TF2 is not rebuilt in hosted CI: it takes about 11,000 requests to logs.tf, whose robots.txt asks crawlers to stay off its API, so it is fetched rarely, slowly and by a person.

**Formats.** Everything above is plain JSON with a declared format:

- `fpsdet.benchmark/1`: the definition: datasets, which are historical, archival classes, what the benchmark can and cannot prove.
- `fpsdet.benchmark-dataset/1`: one dataset's manifest: source, licence, ids, labels, fetch and prepare commands, frozen selection, pinned inputs, and draws where it has them.
- `fpsdet.benchmark-sources/1`: a pinned upstream source list: repository, revision, selection and each file's digest (cs2cd-v2).
- `fpsdet.tf2-sources/1`: tf2-rgl-v2's frozen sources as digests and counts: bans, both selections, every selected log; names nobody.
- `fpsdet.cs2cd-fetch-receipt/1`: what the pinned CS2 fetcher checked, file by file, at which revision.
- `fpsdet.benchmark-result/1`: one run (one draw, for a dataset with draws), every step bound by digest.
- `fpsdet.benchmark-statistics/1`: one draw's evaluation statistics without its rows, bound to the evaluation by digest.
- `fpsdet.benchmark-sensitivity/2`: every predeclared draw of a dataset, in order, with its own inputs, outputs and numbers, and the median and range across them.
- `fpsdet.benchmark-sensitivity/1`: historical: tf2-rgl-v1's published draw beside five public alternative-key draws.
- `fpsdet.benchmark-expected/1`: the pinned identity of every result, per draw for a dataset with draws.
- `fpsdet.benchmark-split/1`: every subject's development, evaluation or baseline membership (never committed for a dataset with public ids).
- `fpsdet.benchmark-capabilities/1`: each technique's claim and the references its evidence is worked out from.
- `fpsdet.benchmark-claims/1`: every public statement of the release, with its class and the artifact it comes from.
- `fpsdet.benchmark-release/1`: the release itself: every dataset version, the expectations, spreads, capabilities, claims and report, by digest.

## 12. The historical record

## Third-party systems

Nothing but fpsdet is evaluated here. A future version can evaluate another system on the same frozen populations without forcing it into fpsdet's schema. It would take one neutral record per subject:

```json
{"format": "fpsdet.benchmark-submission/1", "dataset": "tf2-rgl-v2", "subject": "an id from your own rebuild", "decision": "flag | no_flag | abstain", "observation_kinds": ["optional, the system's own names"], "confidence": null}
```

It would be scored with the same label semantics, splits, eligibility rules (abstain is not a miss) and intervals, and never folded into one number. Not implemented: the immediate goal is that fpsdet itself can be reproduced and checked.
